BRCA1 & BRCA2 — Understanding Your Genes

What these genes do, why mutations matter, and how to interpret your QGene result

What are BRCA1 and BRCA2?

BRCA1 (Breast Cancer gene 1) and BRCA2 (Breast Cancer gene 2) are tumour suppressor genes — they produce proteins that help repair damaged DNA and keep cell growth under control. When either of these genes contains a harmful mutation, the DNA repair process is compromised, significantly increasing the risk of breast, ovarian, and other cancers.

Approximately 1 in 400 people in the general population carry a pathogenic BRCA variant. Among individuals with a strong family history of breast or ovarian cancer, this rises to 1 in 10.

Understanding your QGene result

PATHOGENIC

A pathogenic classification means the variant is predicted to disrupt the normal function of the BRCA gene. This may indicate an increased risk of hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome (HBOC).

This is a research prediction only. It does not constitute a clinical diagnosis.

  • Consult a certified genetic counselor for formal interpretation
  • Consider clinical genetic testing for confirmation
  • Inform first-degree relatives who may be at risk
  • Discuss enhanced screening protocols with your physician
  • Do not make medical decisions based on this tool alone

BENIGN

A benign classification means the variant is predicted not to significantly disrupt BRCA gene function. These variants, sometimes called polymorphisms, are generally not associated with increased cancer risk.

A benign result does not rule out cancer risk from other genetic or environmental factors.

  • Continue routine cancer screening as recommended for your age and sex
  • Benign variants may still affect function in rare cases
  • Family history remains independently important — discuss with your doctor
  • Consider clinical confirmation if there is strong family history
Mutation types explained
Missense

Missense Mutation

A single nucleotide change that results in a different amino acid being incorporated into the protein. Effect depends on the amino acid's role — some are harmless, others critically disrupt function.

Nonsense

Nonsense Mutation

Creates a premature stop codon, causing translation to halt early. The resulting truncated protein is usually non-functional. Nonsense mutations in BRCA genes are typically pathogenic.

Frameshift

Frameshift Mutation

An insertion or deletion that shifts the reading frame of the gene. All downstream amino acids are altered, almost always producing a non-functional protein. High likelihood of pathogenicity.

Silent

Silent Mutation

A nucleotide change that does not alter the amino acid sequence due to codon degeneracy. Generally benign, though some silent variants can affect splicing.

Splice Site

Splice Site Mutation

Occurs at the boundary between exons and introns. Can disrupt normal RNA splicing, leading to inclusion of intronic sequence or exclusion of exonic sequence in the final mRNA.

Conservation

Conservation Score

Measures how conserved a position is across species. A high score (close to 1.0) means the position has barely changed across millions of years of evolution — suggesting it is functionally critical.
